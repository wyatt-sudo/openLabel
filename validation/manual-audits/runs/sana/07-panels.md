# Reader Panels

## For Chronic Illness & Complex Symptoms

Sana is not another loose frequency-wellness product. It has a real FDA De Novo clearance for prescription adjunctive temporary relief of neuropathic pain in adults, and a published sham-controlled neuropathic pain trial. That matters. If you live with chronic pain, the sane interpretation is: this may become a legitimate adjunctive tool for a specific kind of pain, not a general answer to complex illness, PTSD, fibromyalgia, sleep, or recovery unless those claims are separately cleared and evidenced. The hope here is unusually concrete; the boundary still matters.

Evidence basis:
- Saw: FDA De Novo scope, neuropathic pain RCT, PTSD pilot, homepage recovery language, FAQ and clinical pages.
- Means: credible narrow pain substrate; broader uses remain investigational or marketing-adjacent.
- Confidence: medium-high for neuropathic pain scope; medium for broader company posture.
- Gaps: full FDA summary review, MAUDE/adverse-event search, user corpus, pricing/access review.

## For Curious Consumer

Right now Sana says the device is not generally available for sale and is focused on clinical trials, so this is less of an impulse purchase than BrainTap or Healy. If it becomes available to you, the first question is whether you are in the actual indicated population: adult neuropathic pain, using it as an adjunct to other therapy, likely under prescription access. The second question is whether the company makes the practical terms clear: who should avoid it, what it costs, what data it collects, how returns/subscriptions work, and what benefits are realistic.

Evidence basis:
- Saw: device FAQ, return policy, privacy policy, FDA Rx indication.
- Means: consumer risk is currently bounded by availability, but future access design matters.
- Confidence: medium.
- Gaps: current checkout/pricing not available; app onboarding not reviewed.

## For Frontier Health Optimizer

Sana is the kind of frontier tool that deserves a more serious read. Audio-visual stimulation, pain perception, sleep, autonomic settling, and neuroplasticity are real research neighborhoods, and Sana has done more than make a vibe claim: it has a De Novo and a sham-controlled neuropathic pain trial. The discipline question is whether every future use keeps the same standard. PTSD, fibromyalgia, recovery, and well-being should not borrow certainty from neuropathic pain clearance. The frontier survives when each claim keeps its own evidence burden.

Evidence basis:
- Saw: FDA De Novo, BMC Neurology RCT, PTSD pilot, patents/grants/accomplishments, AVS mechanism page.
- Means: promising medical-device frontier; evidence should be segmented by indication.
- Confidence: medium-high for pain; medium for pipeline.
- Gaps: full protocol/results extraction, independent replication, complete patent review.

## For Helpful Healer

If you are a clinician, coach, practitioner, or family member, Sana should be held as an adjunctive prescription pain-relief device, not as a general nervous-system healer. The ethical recommendation is precise: use the cleared indication, screen for light/sound sensitivity and the FDA-listed risks, preserve other modes of therapy, and avoid translating PTSD/fibromyalgia/recovery signals into promises. The strongest version of Sana could help people suffer less without asking them to abandon medical pluralism or self-trust.

Evidence basis:
- Saw: FDA risk list, Rx indication, clinical trial exclusion criteria, safety/FAQ language.
- Means: recommendation quality depends on fit, screening, and scope discipline.
- Confidence: medium-high.
- Gaps: prescribing instructions, current IFU, adverse-event database, clinician materials.

## Will This Offering Survive Agentic Health Management?

Sana survives better than most offerings OpenLabel has looked at because an agent can verify a real regulatory event and a product-specific controlled trial. What the agent will flag is not the core pain indication; it will flag scope hygiene. Homepage recovery language, PTSD halo, older FAQ language saying the device is not approved for any specific condition, return/subscription details, and health-data collection all need to be reconciled into one current, precise story. The remediation path is straightforward: publish an indication-by-indication evidence table, update stale pages, put warnings and data practices where users actually decide, and refuse to let pipeline indications borrow the pain clearance.

Evidence basis:
- Saw: De Novo, RCT, company pages, privacy/return pages, PTSD disclaimer, stale FAQ.
- Means: real substrate survives; messy public translation gets flagged.
- Confidence: medium-high.
- Gaps: complete official FDA review package, MAUDE, ad libraries, current access/pricing.
