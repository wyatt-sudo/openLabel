# Tone and Stance

*The most easily lost nuance in a project like this. Read this before writing any consumer-facing copy. Tone calibration governs every word on the label.*

---

## The audience is suffering

The primary OpenLabel population — people with MCAS, POTS, Long COVID, ME/CFS, dysautonomia, EDS, chronic autoimmune and inflammatory conditions — is **already exhausted** by the time they encounter the tool. They have been dismissed by clinicians, told their symptoms are anxiety, charged for diagnostics that did not help, sold supplements that did not work, and given conflicting advice by every layer of the system that was supposed to help them.

When they encounter OpenLabel, they may have already paid for the offering being audited. They may have bought hope. They may have built a small piece of identity around the practice or community.

**The output must be direct without being dismissive.**

---

## Describe rather than condemn

The label format helps because it describes rather than condemns. This is the central tonal commitment.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| "This is a placebo." | "Also contains: hope and expectancy activation." |
| "This is a scam targeting desperate patients." | "Heavy persuasion pressure — recommend caution." |
| "The mechanism is fake." | "The proposed mechanism is unlikely to be the reason this helps people who feel better. The benefit they report is real; the explanation may be different." |
| "These people are gullible." | (never write this in any form) |
| "The founder is exploiting his customers." | "The marketing register is calibrated for crisis states." |
| "Don't waste your money." | "Lower-cost alternatives are available — including [list]." |

The principle: name the dynamic; do not pass judgment on the people inside it.

---

## Hope deployed honestly is not a grift

An offering that sells hope and delivers hope is not a grift. **Hope and expectancy have real, measurable, psychoneuroimmunological healing effects.** The placebo response is not "nothing"; it is one of the most reliable interventions in medicine.

The harm is not in selling hope. The harm is in selling *mechanism* (a specific causal story about *why* the product helps) while delivering *hope* (a non-specific therapeutic effect that any number of vehicles could activate), then charging mechanism prices and creating mechanism dependency.

OpenLabel must keep this distinction crisp:

- **Real benefit through honest mechanism** — full credit
- **Real benefit through honestly-named non-specific factors** — full credit; this is appropriate care
- **Real benefit through claimed mechanism that is actually non-specific factors** — D10 flags this
- **No benefit, but charged for it** — D1, D2, D3 flag this

A patient who got better on a thing OpenLabel scores poorly on **did not become wrong about getting better.** Their experience is real. We are scoring whether the company is being honest about *why* it happened — not whether *they should have used it*.

---

## Calibrated uncertainty

When we don't know, say we don't know. When evidence is mixed, say it's mixed. When a score is provisional, say it's provisional.

The whole project is about epistemic honesty. **That standard applies to OpenLabel's own outputs.** A consumer-facing tool that performs more certainty than it has earned would be guilty of the same dynamic D6 and D7 are designed to catch in others.

Specifically:

- "Source coverage: provisional" is a real label and appears prominently when triggered.
- "Insufficient evidence to score" is a real verdict — more honest than guessing.
- "We could not find peer-reviewed evidence on this product. Public claims rest on [what we found]." is more honest than "no evidence."
- "Patient signal not yet integrated" is more honest than synthesizing without it.

---

## Unsentimental, but humane

This is the line OpenLabel walks. It is **not** the wellness-industry voice (warm, validating, often vague). It is **not** the debunker voice (smug, dismissive, often cruel). It is closer to a thoughtful pharmacist who likes their patients and tells them what is actually in the bottle.

The internal frameworks were built with deliberately unsentimental language ("Disqualify," "Predatory Apparatus," "Register exploitation"). This vocabulary is correct internally — it keeps us honest about what we are looking at. It is **not** the consumer-facing surface vocabulary. The surface translates the internal precision into language a person in a difficult moment can hear without feeling judged.

---

## What never goes in consumer output

- Personal attacks on founders, employees, or team members
- Mockery of any patient population, even by implication
- Speculation about founder motivation framed as fact
- Brand-voice catchphrases that turn the audit into entertainment ("Caught in the act!" / "Yikes!" / "This one's a doozy")
- Internal scoring jargon without consumer translation (no "Predatory Apparatus" verbatim, no "Register exploitation" verbatim, no naked "Mission Integrity Score 4.05")
- Cosmological dismissal — telling someone their cosmology is wrong rather than naming what the offering is selling within it
- Verdicts that cannot be defended from the source list
- Emoji decoration ⚠ except where intentional and consistent with the visual system

---

## Limits that apply on every surface

Even if a more irreverent or cheeky surface (social-media-shareable mode, etc.) is built later, two limits apply universally:

1. **Mockery of the patient population is never on the table.** On any surface. Ever.
2. **Mockery of the specific marketing dynamic being used can be permissible** in a more irreverent register — but it has to be precise: aim at the dynamic, not at the people caught inside it.

Default to the main-surface tone described above unless an explicit alternate surface is being built and these limits are being honored.

---

## Tone for the chronic illness population specifically

People in this population have been told some version of "your symptoms are not real" or "you are catastrophizing" or "you are anxious" so many times that any whiff of dismissal makes the tool useless to them. **This is the population OpenLabel is built for. They are the stress test for the tone, not an afterthought.**

Specific commitments:

- Never frame symptoms as exaggerated, manufactured, or psychosomatic. (They may be — but the marketing-audit surface is not the place to litigate that.)
- Never frame the use of an offering, even an offering OpenLabel scores poorly, as a personal failure.
- When discussing alternatives, lead with peer-community resources and patient-organization resources before clinical alternatives. The reason: this population's relationship with mainstream medicine is often broken, sometimes for good reason, and "go see a doctor" is not actionable advice for many of them.
- When naming contraindications specific to this population (PEM risk, MCAS triggering, environmental sensitivity exposures, dysautonomia escalation), be specific and concrete. Vague "may not be suitable for some users" is useless.
- Never imply that someone who got worse on an offering should have known better.

---

## The constructive framing

OpenLabel is not primarily a fraud detector. It is a clarity instrument. The end-state we are trying to produce in the consumer is not "now you know this product is bad." It is **"now you can see what you are actually looking at, and you can decide."**

If a person reads an OpenLabel audit and ends up using the offering anyway, with better-calibrated expectations, that is a successful outcome. If a person reads an audit and walks away from a predatory offering, that is also a successful outcome. If a person reads an audit and finds a cheaper or better-evidenced alternative, that is a successful outcome.

**OpenLabel's job is to give people their own agency back, not to make their decisions for them.**

---

## Working-style commitments

When operating on this project:

- **Calibrated uncertainty** — when you don't know, say so. Mixed evidence reads as mixed.
- **Unsentimental assessment** — when something is weak, say so clearly. Soft language to avoid conflict makes the tool worse, not kinder.
- **Momentum over perfection** — a good audit completed now is more valuable than a perfect audit never started.
- **Intellectual engagement** — when an audit reveals a pattern that stretches the methodology, name it. The frameworks are living documents.
- **Autonomous initiative within scope** — propose next steps; surface findings; don't wait for explicit direction once oriented.
